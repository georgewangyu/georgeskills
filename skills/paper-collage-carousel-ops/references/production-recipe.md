# Layered-paper production recipe

Use this as a starting production approach, not a fixed palette or layout.

## Material and layer stack

1. **Base:** use original or licensed scanned paper, or generate a clean material
   plate. Scale grain and folds to the canvas. Keep high-frequency contrast low
   behind reading areas; a noisy overlay does not make a composition handmade.
2. **Paper objects:** create a few distinct torn-edge masks, note shapes and tabs.
   Preserve irregular edge detail and restrained contact shadows. Use a shared
   lighting direction; excessive rotation or deep shadows makes loose stickers.
3. **Meaningful imagery:** select an authentic source crop, owned photo, relevant
   object or original illustration. A cutout hand can direct attention; it should
   not conceal the example. Do not add a generic laptop simply to fill space.
4. **Type:** place editable headlines and body copy above the material layers.
   Use deliberate line breaks, optical spacing and a restrained hierarchy. Body
   text needs a calm surface. Handwritten accents are accents, not long passages.
5. **Teaching marks:** connect a particular claim to its evidence, annotate an
   actual difference, or emphasize the action. Avoid arbitrary source numbers,
   invented graphs and fake product UI. Cite any real data used in the example.
6. **Identity:** use the creator's established small signature or existing mark.
   Keep an invented mascot, heavy footer or mandatory portrait out of the system
   unless selected deliberately by the creator.

## A useful interior example

For a source-based explainer, show a short authentic passage with a small source
label, highlight the relevant phrase, and connect it to a concise interpretation.
Distinguish the quotation, the source's assertion and the author's inference.
Use enough readable detail to teach the relationship at phone size; move lengthy
citations to the accompanying source record/caption rather than shrinking them
into illegibility. Do not label a proposed workflow as a demonstrated result.

## Controlled iteration

If output looks synthetic, identify the failed layer: material, typography,
composition, illustrative detail, example specificity or wording. Change that
layer and compare the same pair again. Avoid cycling through unrelated palettes,
switching models and declaring improvement without examining actual output.

Useful diagnostic comparisons:

- Rich material assets + editable layout versus geometry-only approximations.
- The same copy with different hierarchy versus different hooks with one layout.
- A creator photo doing explanatory work versus an ornamental portrait sticker.
- A real example versus an abstract concept sketch.

Prefer the smallest comparison that answers the actual objection. Texture is not
required for every brand; apply this treatment only when the brief calls for it.

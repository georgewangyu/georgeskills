# Anti-Generic Writing Checklist

Use this after drafting or revising. The goal is not to make prose plain at all
costs. The goal is to remove patterns that make text sound generated,
performative, or off-voice.

## High-Signal Problems

Fix these on sight unless the source writer clearly uses them:

- Fake launch energy:
  - `I am excited to share`
  - `I shipped a real update`
  - `This is a big step`
  - `I am thrilled to announce`
- Generic importance:
  - `this matters`
  - `this is worth paying attention to`
  - `the interesting part is`
  - `what stood out`
- Empty intensifiers:
  - `real` as puffery
  - `genuine` / `genuinely`
  - `truly`
  - `very`
- AI vocabulary:
  - `delve`
  - `leverage`
  - `unlock`
  - `empower`
  - `seamless`
  - `robust`
  - `cutting-edge`
  - `game-changer`
  - `landscape`
  - `paradigm`
- Formulaic structure:
  - `not just X, but Y`
  - repeated rhetorical questions
  - broad setup before concrete news
  - padded rule-of-three phrasing
  - bullets used because the model is avoiding prose

## Voice Fit Questions

Ask these before returning the draft:

1. Could this opener appear unchanged in a stranger's LinkedIn post?
2. Does the sentence explain a concrete change, or only tell the reader how to
   feel about it?
3. Is the confidence level supported by the source material?
4. Did the revision preserve the writer's actual rhythm?
5. Did the draft copy a surface quirk while keeping a generic structure?
6. Would the writer plausibly say this out loud?

## Rewrite Moves

Prefer these fixes:

- Replace excitement with the concrete change.
- Replace praise with a mechanism, receipt, number, or tradeoff.
- Replace abstract category language with the actual artifact.
- Replace "thought leader" cadence with a direct observation.
- Keep one sharp sentence instead of a padded setup.
- Let uncertainty stay visible when the writer normally admits uncertainty.

## Minimal Diff Rule

For revision tasks, do not rewrite a whole piece only because a few lines are
off. Fix the spans that break voice first, then smooth nearby transitions only
when needed.

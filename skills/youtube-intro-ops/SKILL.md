# YouTube Intro Ops

## Goal

Turn a title-and-thumbnail promise into a short spoken opening that confirms
the click, creates a real problem, and points to a payoff the completed video
can deliver.

Use Ludwig Ahgren's four-P lesson as a creator-neutral structure:

1. **Premise:** explain immediately what the video is about. Treat this as the
   hook and click confirmation.
2. **Problem:** name the obstacle, uncertainty, conflict, or stakes that make
   the premise worth following.
3. **Plug:** optionally direct the viewer to subscribe, a live stream, a
   product, or another action. Omit it when it delays the story or has not been
   requested.
4. **Payoff:** tease the specific answer, result, reveal, decision, or scene
   the video will deliver later.

Read `references/ludwig-four-ps.md` when the user names Ludwig, asks about the
four Ps, or needs source provenance.

## Core Rules

- Adapt mechanics, not another creator's persona, cadence, catchphrases, or
  "YouTuber voice."
- Match the title and thumbnail promise. Do not use the intro to quietly swap
  in a different video.
- State the premise in the first one or two spoken sentences.
- Make the problem a real source of uncertainty. Topic labeling is not a
  problem.
- Tease a payoff only when the planned footage or evidence can satisfy it.
- Never invent totals, purchases, outcomes, stakes, credentials, deadlines,
  or emotional reactions.
- When an experiment is still running, speak in questions and placeholders.
  Record a result-dependent pickup after the evidence exists.
- Keep one dominant open loop. Secondary reveals may support it but should not
  compete with it.
- Prefer a clean transition into the first story beat over "let's get into
  it."

## Workflow

### 1. Lock the click promise

Write one sentence for each:

```text
Title promise: [what earned the click]
Thumbnail promise: [what the image makes the viewer expect]
Video answer: [what the viewer can truthfully know by the end]
```

If these point to different videos, resolve the mismatch before scripting.

### 2. Inventory proof and unknowns

Separate:

- verified facts and footage;
- evidence that will be captured during the story;
- unresolved values requiring placeholders;
- claims or disclosures that require creator approval.

Do not turn an unknown into a dramatic fact. For an unfinished challenge,
promise the measurement method and final comparison, not the desired result.

### 3. Draft the four Ps

Draft one compact line or beat for each applicable P:

```text
Premise: [what is happening]
Problem: [what could complicate, contradict, or change it]
Plug: [optional and removable]
Payoff: [the answer or reveal worth staying for]
```

For a vlog, challenge, or documentary experiment, default to
`Premise -> Problem -> method/proof -> Payoff`. Treat the method or proof object
as support inside the premise/problem beats, not as a fifth mandatory P.

### 4. Write for speech

- Target `20-45 seconds` by default; allow up to `60 seconds` when the story
  needs context the viewer cannot infer.
- Use short clauses and concrete nouns.
- Give the speaker one idea per breath.
- Preserve the creator's natural voice and uncertainty.
- Mark optional lines instead of forcing every useful fact into the opening.
- End on a button that naturally enters beat one.

### 5. Pair spoken and visual proof

For every line, name the safest truthful visual available:

```text
Time | Spoken job | Visual proof | Evidence status
```

Show the premise early. When the source is private or sensitive, use a
sanitized physical object, manual ledger, aggregate card, or neutral scene.

### 6. Run the truth and retention audit

Check:

- Does the first sentence confirm the click?
- Is the problem clear without backstory?
- Is the payoff specific and deliverable?
- Can any line be removed without weakening premise, problem, or payoff?
- Is the plug optional and non-disruptive?
- Does the intro avoid a predetermined conclusion?
- Does the final line flow directly into the first real story action?
- Are private, employer, financial, or customer details appropriately bounded?

## Evidence-Contingent Intros

When the ending depends on future data, prepare two layers:

1. **Pre-observation intro:** promise the question, rules, and evidence.
2. **Post-observation pickup:** add only verified totals or result teases that
   do not spoil the entire journey.

Use placeholders such as `[REAL TOTAL]`, `[VERIFIED DELTA]`, or
`[APPROVED PAY MEASURE]` in private planning artifacts. Never film a fake
number as hypothetical B-roll.

## Output Contract

Return:

```text
Click promise:
- Title:
- Thumbnail:
- End-of-video answer:

Four-P map:
- Premise:
- Problem:
- Plug: [line or omitted]
- Payoff:

Recommended spoken intro:
[20-60 second draft]

Performance map:
- [time range] [spoken job] -> [visual]

Alternate opening line:
[one backup]

Truth gates:
- [placeholder, verification, disclosure, or privacy requirement]
```

If the user asks only for an audit, return the four-P map, the failed checks,
and the smallest revision that fixes them.

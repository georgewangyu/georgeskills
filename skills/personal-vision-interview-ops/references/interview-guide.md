# Personal Vision Interview Guide

Use this guide as an adaptive sequence, not a form to dump on the participant.
Ask one primary question at a time. A standard interview normally uses all
core questions; a quick interview may combine adjacent questions after asking
permission.

## Interview posture

- Stay curious, specific, and nonjudgmental.
- Mirror meaning before adding interpretation.
- Prefer concrete scenes and tradeoffs over aspirational labels.
- Ask for an example when an answer is abstract.
- Offer a tentative read, not a diagnosis.
- Let `I do not know` remain a useful answer.
- Do not treat income, status, productivity, marriage, children, location, or
  any conventional milestone as a universal goal.

## Setup prompt

Ask:

> Would you like a quick, standard, or deep interview? Should the result stay
> in this chat, or may I save a draft to a path you choose? Are there topics
> you want to keep vague, skip, or exclude from the final document?

If the participant already supplied these preferences, acknowledge them
instead of asking again.

## Core sequence

### 1. Values and boundaries

> Which values would you preserve even when doing so costs money, status,
> speed, or convenience? What are you unwilling to sacrifice, and what kinds
> of harm or conduct should remain outside the acceptable solution space?

Useful probes:

- Which value has already cost you something?
- What would a tempting violation look like?
- Is this a real boundary or an aspiration you are still building toward?

### 2. Anti-vision and success traps

> What would "successful but living the wrong life" look like? If a current
> path succeeded wildly, which obligations, identity, environment, health
> cost, relationship cost, or loss of freedom could make that success a trap?

Useful probes:

- Which attractive opportunity would be hardest to decline?
- What early drift signal would tell you the trap is forming?

### 3. The ordinary desired life

> Imagine an ordinary week in a life you would be glad to inhabit years from
> now—not a highlight reel. What are you working on? Who surrounds you? Where
> and how do you live? What are relationships, health, learning, finances,
> hobbies, rest, and uncommitted time like?

Useful probes:

- Walk through one ordinary weekday.
- What is deliberately absent?
- Which part matters even if nobody else sees it?

### 4. Freedom from obligation

> If money, status, and mandatory work were handled, how would you spend your
> time? Which questions, activities, or problems would keep pulling you back
> even when nobody rewarded you?

Useful probes:

- What would you still do after the novelty wore off?
- Which interest is curiosity, and which is identity or obligation?

### 5. Preferred mode of contribution

> Which forms of contribution feel intrinsically worthwhile: discovering,
> building, caring, teaching, creating, communicating, organizing, or
> something else? Which are destinations, and which are supporting
> capabilities?

Useful probes:

- Which part gives energy during the work, not only after recognition?
- Which capability is necessary but not personally central?

### 6. Desired before and after

> Whose life, environment, or understanding do you want to change, and what
> should be observably different afterward?

Useful probes:

- What is true before the work exists?
- What becomes possible afterward?
- What would count as meaningful even at small scale?

### 7. Current role hypothesis

> Where are you most useful now: as a specialist, builder, researcher,
> caregiver, integrator, storyteller, organizer, or some combination? What
> depth must you develop, and what should eventually belong to people with
> stronger specialized ability?

Useful probes:

- Where might breadth be disguising avoidance of depth?
- What evidence would change this role hypothesis?

### 8. Financial enough

> What does financial "enough" protect or enable? Is it a safety threshold,
> autonomy, care for others, capital for a mission, a scoreboard, or something
> else? How would you know if pursuing it had begun to undermine what it was
> supposed to protect?

Useful probes:

- Does a number matter, or does a condition matter?
- Which obligation would money remove, and which might it add?

Do not supply a target on the participant's behalf.

### 9. Horizon ladder

> What would need to be true in roughly three years for the desired life to be
> becoming real? What should be true after one year? Which current efforts are
> genuine bridges, and which are merely adjacent activity?

Useful probes:

- What durable asset or capability should each bridge compound?
- What is the smallest reversible experiment?
- What proxy are you at risk of optimizing?

If the participant cannot answer, preserve the horizon as `Open`.

### 10. Unresolved frontier

> Which important question do you not yet know how to answer but would be
> willing to spend years getting closer to? What uncertainty should remain
> explicit instead of being polished into conviction?

Useful probes:

- Why does this question have durable pull?
- What experience or evidence could sharpen it?

## Adaptive branches

Use at most one branch at a time, and only when it follows from the
participant's answer.

### Identity continuity

Use when the participant raises radical change, migration, legacy, embodiment,
or continuity:

> What would need to remain continuous for you to consider a future version
> of yourself meaningfully the same person? What change would make that feel
> like a successor rather than continuity?

### Relationship tradeoff

Use when ambition and close relationships appear to conflict:

> What relationship commitments should constrain the shape or pace of the
> vision, even when the opportunity is unusually strong?

### Place and community

Use when location, belonging, or environment appears causal:

> Which qualities of place and community help you become the person described
> here? Which environments reliably pull you away from it?

### Health and capacity

Use when the vision assumes sustained energy or high intensity:

> What physical and mental capacity does this life require, and what pace
> would preserve that capacity instead of spending it as fuel?

Do not turn this into medical advice.

## Reflection format after each answer

Use a compact pattern:

```text
What I heard: <participant-grounded meaning>.
Tentative read: <interpretation, only if useful>.
Open or tension: <unresolved point, only if present>.
Next question: <one primary question or one clarifying follow-up>.
```

Vary the wording naturally. Do not recite the labels mechanically.

## Synthesis rules

1. Use the participant's concrete language where it improves recognition.
2. Compress repeated themes without erasing meaningful distinctions.
3. Separate values from strategies and interests from obligations.
4. Trace each horizon claim to a supplied answer.
5. Preserve contradictions when resolving them would require a value choice.
6. Make the anti-vision specific enough to recognize in real decisions.
7. Turn the vision into tests, not commandments.
8. Keep the document revisable without making it so vague that anything fits.

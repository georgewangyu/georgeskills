# Engine Routing

Route by the creative and delivery job, not by the novelty of a tool. Recheck
current export behavior before a batch because presentation and render tools
change.

| Lane | Best for | Required proof | Do not choose when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Remotion or equivalent frame-driven code | Exact narration timing, kinetic type, UI or cards, gauges, cascades, focus/depth, alpha, variants, shared-object geometry | Poster, representative motion proof, first/middle/final frames, real alpha composite when relevant | A static asset is enough or a slide-native tool preserves the approved states more simply |
| Keynote or proven slide-native motion | Two or three approved slide-like states, editable object builds, action paths, shared-element moves, quick human retiming | Exact-resolution movie sample, boundary frames, font fidelity, alpha test if used | Frame-exact data-driven variants, long alpha compositing, or unattended export is required but unproved |
| SVG/HTML/CSS/canvas | Lightweight vector overlays, portable UI-like motion, simple deterministic loops | Browser/version receipt, frame capture, contact sheet | Browser timing cannot be tied to the required frame timebase or the effect needs complex video compositing |
| Marp or another deck tool | Static art direction, storyboards, approved key states, simple transitions when browser capture is proven | Exact slide renders and capture proof | The deck would become the animation authority without tested motion export |
| Manim | Equations, coordinates, geometry, graphs, algorithms, semantic mechanisms | Conceptual-accuracy review plus first/middle/final frames | The job is primarily editorial type, product-like UI, cards, or brand motion |
| Sketch diagram lane | Mostly static social diagram with one calm loop cue | Poster and loop-boundary check | Premium editorial choreography, depth, focus, or multi-state continuity is required |
| Source-faithful screen or receipt | Real product behavior, benchmarks, articles, claims, or evidence | Provenance, readable crop, exact claim fit | The visual is illustrative or the real source cannot support the claim |
| Generated image or video | Atmosphere, metaphor, impossible B-roll, clearly synthetic transitions | Synthetic label, prompt/provider record, artifact inspection | The asset could be mistaken for evidence, product behavior, or a real event |
| Blender or another 3D tool | Bespoke geometry, camera, lighting, simulation, reusable 3D scenes | Low-resolution scene proof and preserved editable source | The request did not explicitly opt into the additional 3D burden |

## Decision Sequence

1. Ask whether motion carries meaning. If not, use A-roll, source proof, or a
   static asset.
2. Ask whether the asset proves a real claim. If yes, lead with source-faithful
   proof; editorial motion may frame it but not replace it.
3. Ask whether exact semantic transformation is mathematical, geometric,
   graph-based, algorithmic, or mechanism-critical. If yes, route to Manim.
4. Ask whether the approved design is essentially two or three slide-like
   states and human editability matters most. If yes, test a slide-native lane.
5. Ask whether exact timebase, alpha, variants, or coded geometry matters. If
   yes, use Remotion or equivalent frame-driven code.
6. Use browser vector motion only when its capture path is deterministic and
   simpler than the alternatives.

No lane wins from a successful export alone. Choose the lane that preserves
the approved key states, hits the narration, survives the editor handoff, and
can be inspected at the real boundary.

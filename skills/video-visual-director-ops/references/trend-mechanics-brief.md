# Trend Mechanics Brief

Analyze editing references as reusable mechanics, not as a creator-style imitation request.

For each reference, capture:

- source URL, platform, post date, current views, likes, comments, and account size when available
- whether performance is a one-off or repeats across several posts or creators
- first-frame composition and the first two seconds
- cut cadence and average shot length
- A-roll scale changes, crops, reframes, and camera movement
- foreground/background depth, masks, cutouts, and occlusion
- typography, caption hierarchy, highlight behavior, and safe-area use
- B-roll or proof-receipt placement and dwell time
- sound-effect, music-beat, and transition synchronization
- recurring motif or visual grammar
- likely production method and Remotion feasibility
- saturation risk: emerging, established, overused, or stale
- portable mechanics to borrow
- elements to avoid because they are identity-specific, deceptive, illegible, or too expensive

Record a `mechanic_budget` before recommending a treatment. Default to `1` for
a short-form video unless the creator asks for more. End with a short
recommendation that stays within that budget:

1. the chosen mechanic or mechanics worth testing in this video
2. one mechanic to omit
3. the exact beats where each test belongs
4. a low-cost implementation path and a higher-upside alternative

Never claim that a format is trending without current evidence. Never reproduce the exact recognizable style of a living creator; translate mechanics into the project's own visual language.
